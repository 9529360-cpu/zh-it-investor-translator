# Native Italian Chat Style

Use this reference for conversational Italian, especially WhatsApp-style client messages, shorthand, punctuation, and relationship tone.

## Goal

Produce Italian that feels written in a real conversation, not translated from Chinese and not copied from a business-letter template.

## Message rhythm

- Prefer one clear idea per sentence.
- Use short sentences for short source messages.
- Omit subjects when Italian naturally omits them.
- Use connective words only when they add a real relationship between ideas.
- Avoid turning every comma in Chinese into a comma in Italian.
- Do not repeat the same noun or courtesy phrase when Italian would naturally omit it.
- Keep punctuation proportional to the sender's tone.

## Modern formal client chat

For Lei register, prefer natural verb-based politeness:

- `può...`
- `ha avuto modo di...`
- `sta seguendo...`
- `preferisce...`
- `le invierò...`
- `mi scriva pure...`

Avoid needlessly ceremonial wording such as:

- `La prego di voler...`
- `Sarei lieta di comunicarLe...`
- `Qualora fosse di Suo gradimento...`
- repeated capitalized `Le/Sua/Sue` in ordinary chat

Traditional capitalization is not wrong, but it often feels stiff in modern messaging. Use lowercase courtesy clitics and possessives by default unless the user asks for formal correspondence.

## Tu register

When the relationship is clearly informal:

- use `tu` forms consistently;
- do not retain Lei-style distance because the Chinese source says `您` if the established Italian conversation has already switched to `tu`;
- keep the same degree of familiarity as the conversation rather than making the message suddenly intimate.

## WhatsApp realism

Natural chat may use:

- shorter sentence boundaries;
- omitted subjects;
- a light `allora`, `comunque`, `capisco`, or `magari` when functionally useful;
- emojis already present in the source or conversation.

Do not force abbreviations, slang, emojis, exclamation marks, or discourse markers to simulate authenticity.

## Incoming Italian shorthand

Interpret common forms naturally:

- `nn` -> `non`
- `x` -> `per`
- `xché / xke / xk` -> `perché`
- `cmq` -> `comunque`
- `qnd` -> `quando`
- `qlc / qlcs` -> `qualcuno / qualcosa` depending on context
- `msg` -> `messaggio`
- `info` -> `informazioni`
- repeated punctuation and emojis express tone rather than additional factual meaning

When translating to Chinese, translate the intended message, not the abbreviation itself.

## Common Chinese discourse fillers

Do not mechanically translate repeated:

- `好的`
- `明白`
- `了解了`
- `没关系`
- `其实`
- `那么`
- `那`

Choose whether Italian needs an equivalent at all. If it does, select it by conversational function.

## Naturalness examples

Less natural: `Può effettuare investimenti in base alle Sue preferenze di investimento.`

Prefer: `Può decidere come investire in base alle sue preferenze.`

Less natural: `Ha seguito le azioni che abbiamo raccomandato attualmente?`

Prefer: `Ha avuto modo di valutare qualcuno dei titoli che abbiamo segnalato di recente?`

Less natural: `Io continuerò come al solito a fare push delle informazioni.`

Prefer: `Continuerò a inviarle gli aggiornamenti come sempre.`
